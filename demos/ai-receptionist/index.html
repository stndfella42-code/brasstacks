Contractor call script

Approved sequence
Disclose“Thanks for calling [Business]. I’m the virtual receptionist. Are you calling about a new project, existing job, or urgent service issue?”
SafetyAsk only the approved emergency screen for the trade. Route life-safety issues to emergency services or the contractor’s on-call path. Never diagnose.
IdentifyCollect full name, callback number, service address, ownership status, and preferred contact method.
QualifyCapture project type, symptoms or scope, timeline, equipment or property context, service-area fit, and agreed budget qualifier when appropriate.
BookOffer only approved calendar windows. Confirm who will be present, access instructions, and the exact next step.
CloseRepeat the date and arrival window, say what the contractor will receive, and avoid promising price, diagnosis, or same-day completion.

Client onboarding

Launch checklist
  • Map service areas, job types, disqualifiers, business hours, holidays, and escalation contacts.
  • Write one call flow per major intent: new estimate, existing customer, emergency, vendor, and wrong number.
  • Connect the calendar with protected buffers, travel zones, and owner-approved appointment types.
  • Confirm call recording, consent, privacy, retention, and AI disclosure requirements with qualified counsel for the client’s jurisdictions.
  • Run scripted test calls, adversarial edge cases, voicemail fallback, transfer failure, and duplicate-booking tests.
  • Launch after hours first, review every call for two weeks, then expand only after acceptance criteria are met.

A reasonable planning reserve is 3–4.5 hours per client each month during a stable period, with more time during launch. Track the real number before promising “passive” revenue.

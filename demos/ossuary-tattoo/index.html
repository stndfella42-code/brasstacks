§ 00 — The standard

We don't chase trends. We tattoo things that will still be worth wearing when you're eighty.

I

Drawn for you

Custom work is drawn from scratch and never repeated. Your piece doesn't go back in a binder for the next person who walks in.

II

Clinical where it counts

Single-use needles, medical-grade barrier film, and an autoclave that's spore-tested monthly. The room is dark. The station is spotless.

III

Honest, not agreeable

If an idea will blur, fade, or age badly at the size you want, we'll tell you, and then draw you something better.

§ 04 — Aftercare

Heal it
right.

Half of a great tattoo is what you do over the next four weeks. Follow this and it heals sharp. Ignore it and no artist on earth can save it.

  1. First 24 hours

    Leave the wrap on.

    We seal it with a medical film. Keep it on a full day, even if it fills with ink and plasma. That's normal. Peel it off slowly under warm running water.

  2. Days 2–7

    Wash. Dry. Thin layer.

    Wash twice a day with unscented soap and clean hands. Pat dry with a paper towel, never a bath towel. Rub in a rice-grain amount of unscented balm.

  3. Weeks 2–4

    Let it flake.

    It will peel and itch like a sunburn. Tap it, don't scratch it. Switch to a fragrance-free lotion once or twice a day.

  4. Forever

    Sunscreen. Always.

    UV is what kills tattoos. Put SPF 50 on healed work whenever it sees daylight. Touch-ups are free within 90 days if anything heals light.

For two weeks, never:

  • Soak it. No baths, pools, hot tubs, lakes, or ocean.
  • Put it in direct sun or a tanning bed.
  • Re-wrap it in plastic after the first day.
  • Use petroleum jelly, scented lotion, or anything with alcohol.
  • Let shared gym equipment or your pets rub against it.

Something look wrong? Text the aftercare line any time: (303) 555-0113. If you have spreading redness or a fever, see a doctor first, then let us know.
